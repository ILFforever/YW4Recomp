How it works

Online

Press Online in Ark Swap or Blasters as on a Switch. Instead of Nintendo's network, which only a Switch with a Nintendo account can join, the game reaches one of our servers and finds the other players there. Local stays local, as on a Switch.

Under the hood it is the game's own local wireless play, carried over the internet: the same menus, rooms and rules, with nothing to install or sign up for. Thanks to Quandiy, who built the first version of it in their fork.

Ark Swap

Trading
  1. Open Ark Swap from the Yo-kai Pad and choose Swap Online.
  2. One player picks Recruit by myself; the other waits for the room in the list and joins it.
  3. Pick the Yo-kai to offer and swap. The new Yo-kai arrives with its own scene.

Every screen of the trade works with the mouse: point at a room, the wheel moves through the list, a click picks. The room list's hint bar shows F4 Network Settings (R on a controller) to change the server or the room code without leaving.

Blasters

Missions

Form a team of up to four and take on missions together, with no delay between the players' games. In the team room, Online starts a team or finds one; the rest is the game as it was.

  • Chat (X) moves on W A S D, its tabs on your menu keys, and holds your character still while it's open.
  • The mission's R menu works with the mouse; the camera is yours again the moment it closes.
  • Your battle keys work in missions, and buttons you didn't give a battle key use your menu key, so no hint shows N/A.

Server and room codes

Overlay

The overlay's Online tab holds the settings: the server, each with its live ping and the nearest marked; Ark Swap and Blasters each Public or Private with a code; your player name and whether Discord shows what you're doing.

  • Play with a friend: both of you pick the same server. Players on different servers never meet.
  • Public: everyone on the server sees your room.
  • Private: only players with the same code do. Codes count upper and lower case: KEY123 and key123 are different rooms.

The settings lock while you're in a room, and change freely before: the next search uses them.

Our servers and your privacy

Servers

Two small servers, in the US East and in Singapore, pass the games' packets between the players of a room; the games themselves run on your PCs. Game packets go the fast way (UDP), with the slower, sure way (TCP) when a network blocks it.

  • No IP addresses kept: the servers' logs name each connection by a hash that changes at every restart, and are deleted after 7 days.
  • Nothing else stored: no accounts, no saves, no chat; a room is gone when its players leave.
  • Fair use: limits per address keep one person from filling a server.
