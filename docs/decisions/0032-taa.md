# 0032 — Temporal anti-aliasing from the game's own motion vectors

Status: accepted, first version (2026-10-27). The owner picked TAA as the next graphics feature, ahead of steadier
shadow cascades (which turned out to need new cascades, not snapping: the game's are perspective-warped).

## Context

FXAA and SMAA (`gpu_aa.c`) smooth the edges of one frame. They can't fix shimmer: thin things (fences, the goal
net, wires, leaves, shadow edges) that fall between pixels differently every frame, so they crawl while the camera
moves. TAA does: the 3D draws are shifted by a different fraction of a pixel every frame, and each frame is blended
into a running history, so a pixel ends up averaging many sample positions.

TAA needs three things: the shift (jitter), where each pixel was last frame (motion), and a place in the frame
before the UI.

**The game already writes motion.** The G-buffer's first target (RG16F) holds each pixel's screen position this
frame minus last frame's, in 0..1 screen units (fragment program `a93492`: current position from the per-object
`mtxLP`, last frame's from the per-object last world and the camera's last view-projection in `cb1` rows 32-35;
decision 0026). The game puts this frame's world in the "last world" slot for most objects, so the motion is
mostly the camera's; moving characters get no motion of their own.

## Decision

1. **Jitter as a viewport shift, only on 3D objects.** `gpu_dynamic_state` adds the frame's shift (Halton 2,3, 8
   positions, in pixels) to the viewport of draws whose vertex program has the engine's local-matrix block in slot 2
   (`CBUSE_UB_LOCAL_MATRIX_IDX`, the 3D objects) into a target of the scene's size. Not through the projection:
   the motion then stays free of jitter (both of its positions come from unshifted matrices), and full-screen passes
   (lighting, the shadow mask, post effects) are not shifted, so they read the G-buffer at the right texels.
   Draws after this frame's TAA (the UI, 23 draws in town) are never shifted. In town ~420-450 draws per frame are.
2. **The motion is copied when the G-buffer pass ends.** The game uses the image for something else later in the
   frame (read at the composite it held other content), so `gpu_taa_targets_end` copies it into our own image at the
   first render-target change after a G-buffer draw.
3. **The resolve goes where FXAA and SMAA go**, after the bloom composite (`58b87f44`) and before the UI, in the
   same reversible perceptual curve (`taa_resolve.frag`): history fetched at the pixel's last position (Catmull-Rom,
   5 taps), clipped toward a variance box of the 3x3 neighbourhood in YCoCg (so disoccluded or self-moving things
   don't leave trails), blended with 10% of the new frame (up to 25% when moving fast). The longest motion of the
   3x3 is used, so the edges of moving things follow them. A frame without TAA in between, a size change or no
   motion this frame starts the history over.
4. **A light sharpen on output** (`taa_output.frag`, `YW4_TAA_SHARPEN`, default 0.5): each pixel pushed from its
   4 neighbours' average, clamped to their range (no rims). Blending frames softens the picture; the owner prefers
   sharp textures.
5. **One option in the existing setting**: ANTI-ALIASING gets a fourth choice, TAA (`YW4_AA=3`). Default AA stays off.

## How it was checked

- `YW4_AA_DEBUG=4` shows the motion (x red, y green, x50): turning the camera gives a smooth sideways field over the
  world, zero on the player and Whisper (they move with the camera), zero in the sky (no G-buffer there).
- Direction of the motion: `YW4_AA_DEBUG=5` shows how far the moved history is from the new frame. Mean over the
  world on a turning frame: signs (+x, -y) 18.8, (+x, +y) 21.6, (-x, ±y) 53, no motion at all 43. (+x, -y) is what
  the code picks from the window origin (upper left).
- The goal net in the schoolyard: without AA it breaks into dots and dashes; with TAA it is a continuous mesh.
- Running player during the town walk: no visible trails.

## Not done yet

- Per-object motion (characters walking on their own get the camera's motion only; the clamp hides the trail).
  The takeover draws know last frame's world for their objects (decision 0030) and could write true motion.
- The GPU cost was not measured on its own (two full-screen passes and one copy per frame).
- Effects drawn without the local-matrix block (particles, some forward-drawn effects) are not jittered: they get no
  anti-aliasing from TAA, but are not harmed either.
