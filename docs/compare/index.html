    Resolution: the Switch and 4K

    Graphics

    The Switch draws the world at 1600×900; here it can go up to 3840×2160, 5.8 times the pixels. The menus and text are drawn at the same size on top. Same moment, only the resolution changed (anti-aliasing off in both); the 1600×900 shot is stretched to 4K the way a 4K screen would show it.

    Setting
    F1 Display › Resolution
    Switch
    1600×900
    Options
    1080p, 1440p, 4K
    4K speed
    90+ frames/s on an RTX 4070 laptop with the other features off; more with upscaling

    Shadow map size

    Graphics

    A bigger shadow map gives sharper, more detailed shadow edges, most visibly far from the camera. The bamboo forest with soft shadows off, so only the map size changes.

    Meant to be used with soft shadows (PCSS): a sharp 4096 map alone shows every leaf as a hard dot; soft shadows on top keep the detail and soften it with distance, as in the next section.

    Setting
    F1 Shadows › Shadow map
    Switch
    1024
    Recommended
    4096 with soft shadows ×2
    !

    Soft shadows (PCSS)

    Graphics

    Percentage-closer soft shadows behave more like real ones. Shown with the shadow map at 4096.

    • Sharp at the contact: feet, poles, the base of a wall.
    • Softer farther away: tree canopies become dappled shade, not hard dots.
    • Less shimmer on distant shadows while the camera moves.
    Setting
    F1 Shadows › Soft shadows
    Shown with
    Shadow map 4096
    Recommended
    ×2

    Contact shadows

    Graphics

    The shadow map can't see small, close detail. Contact shadows fill it in from the picture itself: a short ray from each point toward the sun finds what's in the way.

    • Small objects cast their shadow: the taps over the schoolyard sink.
    • Depth in foliage: hedge and flower-bed leaves shade each other.
    • Thin lines under ledges: balconies, a window's awning, a wall's cap.
    Setting
    F1 Shadows › Contact shadows
    Quick flip
    F7
    Default
    On
    GPU cost, 4K
    +0.04 to 0.25 ms
    Limit
    only what's on screen casts

    Anti-aliasing

    Graphics

    Applied to the 3D scene before the menus are drawn, so text stays sharp. TAA also stops thin things (fences, nets, wires) from shimmering while the camera moves. Yo-kai World, zoomed in 3×.

    Setting
    F1 Anti-aliasing
    Switch
    None
    Options
    FXAA, SMAA, TAA, FSR, DLSS

    Texture filtering

    Graphics

    Without it the ground blurs a short way ahead. Anisotropic filtering keeps the road, pavement and walls sharp into the distance, even at a low angle. Yo-kai World, zoomed in 2×.

    Setting
    F1 Detail › Texture filtering
    Switch
    Off
    Recommended
    ×16, almost no cost

    Performance
